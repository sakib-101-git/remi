# How cross-border transfers work

## The basic journey
A cross-border transfer has three steps: your money is collected in the sending currency, it is converted at some exchange rate, and it is paid out in the receiving currency to the recipient's account. Each step can add cost or delay.

## Rails and channels
Bank wires use correspondent banking networks. They handle large amounts and are widely accepted for tuition, but they are usually slower (often several business days) and more expensive. Digital remittance providers hold money in local accounts in many countries, so they can pay out quickly and cheaply, but they often have lower per-transfer limits. Tuition-specific payment platforms work directly with institutions and usually require supporting documents.

## Business days and cut-off times
Providers process transfers on business days. If you submit after a provider's daily cut-off time, processing starts the next business day. Weekends and public holidays add delay. Remi uses the worst-case delivery date and keeps a safety buffer before any deadline.

## Why amounts are checked
Every regulated provider monitors transfers for fraud and financial crime. Larger or unusual transfers may be held for checks or require documents. This is normal and protects you.

## Choosing a provider
Compare landed cost (fee plus FX markup), the worst-case arrival date, whether the provider supports your corridor and amount, and whether the recipient needs the exact amount. Remi ranks options on these factors and explains why the others were rejected.
