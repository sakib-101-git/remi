# FX and fee glossary

## Mid-market rate
The mid-market rate is the midpoint between the buy and sell prices that banks use to trade currency with each other. It is the fairest reference point for any conversion, and it is the rate you see on most financial news sites. Remi always shows the mid-market rate next to the rate a provider offers, so the gap is visible.

## Offered rate and FX markup
The offered rate is the rate a provider actually gives you. Most providers set it slightly worse than mid-market and keep the difference. That difference is the FX markup. A 1.2% markup on a 1,000,000 BDT transfer costs about 12,000 BDT, even when the provider advertises "zero fees".

## Upfront fee
A fixed or percentage charge shown at checkout. Some providers charge a flat amount (for example a fixed wire fee), others charge a percentage of the amount sent, and some combine both.

## Landed cost
Landed cost is the total cost of a transfer in the sending currency: the upfront fee plus the FX markup. Remi calculates it as: landed cost = amount sent − (amount received ÷ mid-market rate). Comparing landed cost is the only reliable way to compare providers, because a low fee can hide a wide markup.

## Effective cost percentage
Landed cost divided by the amount sent. It lets you compare a small transfer with a large one.

## Correspondent and intermediary fees
Traditional wires can pass through intermediary banks that deduct their own fees, so the recipient may receive less than expected. For payments that must arrive in full (like tuition), choose a channel that guarantees the received amount, or use a reverse quote.

## Forward and reverse quotes
A forward quote answers "if I send X, how much arrives?". A reverse quote answers "how much must I send so exactly Y arrives?". Reverse quotes are ideal for invoices with a fixed amount due.
