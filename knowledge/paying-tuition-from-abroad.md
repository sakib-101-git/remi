# Paying tuition from abroad: timing and deadlines

## Work backwards from the deadline
Find the tuition due date on your invoice, then subtract the provider's worst-case delivery time and a safety buffer of at least two business days. That gives the last safe day to send. Remi does this automatically and marks providers that cannot make the deadline as infeasible.

## Make sure the full amount arrives
Universities usually expect the exact invoiced amount. If intermediary fees reduce the amount received, your account may show a balance owing. Use a channel that guarantees the received amount, and use a reverse quote to calculate how much to send.

## Large amounts and limits
Tuition is often larger than the per-transfer limit of low-cost remittance apps. Splitting a payment into several smaller transfers to get under a limit is not a workaround: it can look like structuring and may cause your transfers to be blocked. Use a channel that supports the full amount, such as a tuition payment platform or a bank wire with supporting documents.

## Verify the payee
Only use payment details from your university's official portal or invoice that you obtained yourself. Be very suspicious of emails saying the bank details have changed. Remi only pays institutions whose details match its verified registry.
