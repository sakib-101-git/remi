# Tuition payment documentation checklist

## What banks and payment platforms commonly ask for
When you pay tuition from abroad, the sending bank or platform usually needs to confirm that the payment is genuinely for education and that the money comes from a legitimate source. Requirements differ by country and institution, but the following documents are commonly requested:

- An offer letter, admission letter or enrolment confirmation from the institution.
- The tuition invoice showing your student ID, the amount due and the institution's official payment details.
- A copy of your passport and, where relevant, your student visa or study permit.
- A source-of-funds declaration, for example a letter from the family member paying, a bank statement, a scholarship letter or an education-loan sanction letter.
- In some corridors, a specific form or purpose-of-payment code for education remittances.

## Practical tips
Use the institution's payment details exactly as printed on the official invoice or student portal, and include your student ID as the payment reference. Keep copies of everything and the transfer receipt. Start early: document checks can add days.

## Illustrative only
This checklist is illustrative and written for the Remi sandbox. Real requirements vary by bank, country and institution and can change. Always confirm with your sending bank and your university's finance office, and check official sources.
